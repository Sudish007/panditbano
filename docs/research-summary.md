## 1. Tradition baseline (§12.1) — the sentences the site uses

1. **Default**: "This site follows the Shukla Yajurveda (Vajasaneyi Madhyandina) tradition with the Paraskara Grihya Sutra as the backbone of household rites — the tradition most Hindi-belt family purohits follow." Evidence: R1 M1–M6 (Wikipedia *Yajurveda*, *Shakha*, *Madhyandina Shakha*: Madhyandina "currently recited by all over North Indian Brahmins"; Oldenberg: PGS "belongs to the White Yagur-veda and forms an appendix to Kâtyâyana's Srauta-sûtra"; SSVV lists शुक्लयजुर्वेद and पौरोहित्य as subjects).
2. **Two-layer structure stated plainly** (R1 §C4, M15): the Vedic core (PGS procedure + VS mantras) is wrapped in the Puranic paddhati frame (Ganesh-Ambika pujan, kalash sthapana, shodasha-matrika, navagraha, punyahavachana, nandi-shraddha, raksha-sutra, kanyadaan sankalpa text) and in lokachara (tilak, jaimala, granthi-bandhan, sindoor, mangalsutra, kohbar, matkor). Each step record carries `layer: "vedic" | "paddhati" | "lokachara"` so the UI can label it; PGS has none of the paddhati/lokachara items (R1 §C4 INFERENCE from the kandika list, confirmed by acharyasiyaramdas.com for mangalsutra/garland/granthi — FACT M15).
3. **Warrant for variants** (R1 M7): every "regional variants" block quotes PGS I.8.11–13 — ग्रामवचनं च कुर्युः … तस्मात्तयोर्ग्रामः प्रमाणमिति श्रुतेः ("at weddings and funerals authority rests with the village") — with the Harihara/Gadadhara gloss extending it to देशाचार/कुलाचार/जात्याचार. Variants are "variants", never "corruptions" (also R4 §8.3 rule 8).
4. **Where the regions deviate** (R1 §B2, §J, M8, M13, M36–M40): Mithila — ≈ 75 % Vajasaneyi / ≈ 25 % Chhandog (Samaveda; Shandilya gotra) households, so the site never asserts PGS for every Maithil family; Mithila sequence Panji → Siddhant → Nishchaitambul → Matkor → Parichhan/Dwarachar → Kanyadaan → **Sindoor-daan (the central rite)** → Saptapadi → Kohbar → Chaturthi → Dwiragaman. Bhojpuri belt — Madhyandina/PGS core plus tilak, matkor (women dig clay with songs; spade worshipped with sindoor; five women's खोईंछा), madwa with kalash **and a Satyanarayan katha**, dwar-puja (var-pujan, दुर्गा-जनेऊ, चढ़ाव, तागपाट), kohbar, माँग-बहोरन, chhathi/barahi. Rajasthan — Madhyandina Shrimali purohits; 4 phere; pithi-dastur, toran-marna, hathleva; rasam-pagri strongest. Punjab/Haryana Hindu — 7 phere with vows (secondary source), bhaat/mayra, chooda-kalire; the purohits' Veda shakha not established (UNCERTAIN → the site says "practice varies"). Pahari — Kumaoni "Aanchal" vivah, suwal-pathai, dhuliarghya; Garhwal baan/haldi-haat. Awadh/Braj/Bundelkhand — mainstream (Braj Gaur Samaj page confirms 4 bhanwar; Bundelkhand report speaks of 7).
5. **Pronunciation note for Level 1** (R1 M41): North-Indian Vedic pandits often pronounce ष as ख; the site labels it a regional feature, not an error.
6. **Paddhati shelf** the site cites as bibliography, with the brief's two title corrections (R1 §H1, M32): Gita Press *Nitya Karma Puja Prakash* (code 592), *Nitya Karma Prayoga* (139), *Sanskar Prakash* (2033 — not "Shodash Sanskar Vidhi"), *Vivah Sanskar Paddhati* (2191), *Upanayan Sanskar Paddhati* (2183), *Antyakarma-Shraddha Prakash* (1593 — not "Antyeshti Karma Paddhati"), *Tripindi Shraddha Paddhati* (1928), *Satyanarayan Vrat Katha* (1367), *Rudrashtadhyayi* (1627), *Garud Puran Saroddhar* (1416), *Durga Saptashati* (876), *Panchang Pujan Paddhati* (2228). "Karmakand Bhaskar"/"Karmakand Pradeep" online are **Gayatri Pariwar (AWGP)** editions and are labelled as that separate reformist tradition (M33); *Karmakand Pradeep: Dashakarmadi Paddhati* (Devnarayan Sharma, Bharatiya Vidya Prakashan 2024) is cited by its real publisher; the brief's Khemraj/Chaukhamba attribution is dropped as unconfirmed (UNCERTAIN). Dayanand's *Sanskar Vidhi* is labelled Arya Samaj (M34). A Madhyandina mantra compendium exists: *Madhyandiniya Karmakanda Mantra Sangrah* (Gopal Chandra Mishra) (R1 §H2).

## 2. Occasion-level decisions (§12.2–12.5)

### 2.1 Vivaha (R1 §D; R2 §5.4)
- Mainstream sequence = the 28-item index of the Dwivedi 2020 Madhyandina pothi (R1 §D1, source `ia-vivah-mvf`): var-varan/tilak → purvanga pujan (mandap, kalash, haridralepan, kankan, kohbar, matrika, lokachara) → dev-pitru nimantran → nandimukh shraddha → barat → dwar-puja → vivah prarambh (madhuparka…) → agni-sthapan → granthi-bandhan → shakhochchar/gotrochchar → **kanyadaan** (sankalpa, godan, panigrahana) → **vivah homa** (laja-homa, ashmarohana, 4th laja-homa, 4th parikrama, prajapatya) → **saptapadi** → surya/dhruva darshan, hridayalambhan → **sindoor-daan (sumangali)** → granthi-bandhan → abhisheka/ashirvad → uttar-pujan → acharya dakshina → kankan-mokshan → three-night rules → **chaturthi-karma**.
- Bhanwar block as R1 §D2/M9–M11: brother divides the khil into four; rounds 1–3 "आगे वधू पीछे वर"; granthi-bandhan before round 4; round 4 "वर आगे वधू पीछे हो कर चुपचाप". The site presents 4 as the living Madhyandina paddhati, with one note that Harihara calls the 4th round custom (समाचारात् तूष्णीं) and that one modern scholar argues for three (R1 §D3).
- "7 phere": one explanatory paragraph — the 4 parikramas + 7 steps merged in popular usage; Rajasthan/Gujarat/Mithila/Arya Samaj keep 4; Punjabi Hindu and some UP/Bundelkhand families speak of 7 (M13, INFERENCE, phrased as such).
- Sindoor-daan: after saptapadi, surya-darshan, dhruva-darshan and hridaya-sparsha; bride to the groom's left; groom's father takes sindoor from the purohit, 5 pinches for the kuladevata; applied with the ring finger or a gold ring; mantra सुमङ्गलीरियं वधूः… (M14 — the mantra attachment is paddhati practice, not PGS; the record says so). Mangalsutra: `layer: "lokachara"`, note "not in the Grihya Sutras; the Hindi-belt suhag marker is sindoor" (M15).
- Regional appendices (P2): Maithil, Bhojpuri, Rajasthani, Pahari (Kumaon/Garhwal), Punjabi/Haryanvi, Awadhi/Braj/Bundelkhandi — contents per R1 §J.

## 4. Transliteration scheme (brief §6.1; R2 §2 font note; R3 §9.5; R4 §10; R5 §4.3)

1. **Devanagari is the single source of truth.** The `devanagari` field holds the source reading with svara marks when the source has them (`svara: true`), else `svara: false` and the UI says "svara marks not shown". Allowed code points (brief §6.1 **extended by R2's FACT**): U+0900–097F, danda/double danda U+0964/0965, avagraha U+093D, Devanagari digits, svara U+0951–0954, **Vedic Extensions U+1CD0–1CFF**, **Devanagari Extended U+A8E0–A8FF** (the Wikisource SYV svara text and the sasvara Rudrashtadhyayi both use U+A8F3 ꣳ, U+1CEA and U+1CED — R2 §2 note, C10; R1 §K 12.7), ZWJ/ZWNJ U+200C/200D, and the pluta digit ३ inside ओ३म्. The content-gate Devanagari-only test (brief §6.6) must allow U+A8E0–A8FF or every Shukla-YV mantra fails.
2. **IAST is generated at build time** from the Devanagari with `@indic-transliteration/sanscript` (npm, **MIT**, v1.3.3 — verified in §A), Devanagari → IAST, deterministic, round-trip tested on a fixture set. Svara marks and ꣳ are stripped before transliteration (IAST carries no accent); the Yajurvedic ꣳ is rendered as ṃ (recorded as a converter rule, since sanscript has no mapping for U+A8F3 — INFERENCE, the fixture test proves it).
3. **Easy Roman** is derived from IAST by a fixed table (brief §6.1): ā→aa, ī→ee, ū→oo, ṛ→ri, ṝ→ree, ḷ→li, ṃ→m, ḥ→h, ṅ→n, ñ→n, ṭ→t, ḍ→d, ṇ→n, ś→sh, ṣ→sh, ai/au unchanged, ch for c, chh for ch. (The brief lists ā→aa, ṣ/ś→sh, ṭ→t, ṇ→n, ṃ→m, ḥ→h, ṛ→ri; the rest fill the table consistently.)
4. **Display**: one persisted segmented control `IAST / easy Roman / off` in the verse toolbar, state shown on the control (R3 §4 point 3). IAST lines are set in Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit's own Latin so the Devanagari and its transliteration share baseline and colour (R3 §9.5); the one missing glyph ḹ (U+1E39) falls back to Noto Sans — a build-time check counts ḹ in the corpus (R3 §11).
5. **Language tagging** (R4 §10, §7.2): Sanskrit spans `lang="sa"`, IAST and easy-Roman spans `lang="sa-Latn"`, Hindi words inside English prose `lang="hi"`, Bhojpuri `lang="bho"`. Screen readers get a svara-less text via `aria-label` on each accented line (R4 §7.3).
6. **UI and English prose use easy Roman** for names (Ganesh, Satyanarayan, shraddha, havan, sankalpa), IAST only inside the verse view (R5 §4.3 S2). Term ids are ASCII slugs of the easy-Roman form (`vivah`, `pind-daan`, `matkor`) (R5 S3).
7. **Danda handling** (R3 §5 point 8, C17): the renderer joins `।`/`॥` and the verse number to the preceding word with U+00A0 or `white-space: nowrap` on the terminal span, so no danda or number ever sits alone on a line (vedabase and bhagavadgita.com both fail this).

### 7.4 Wording rules that the content gate lints (R4 §5.3–5.5; brief §1)
Site voice never asserts an outcome; the frame is `परम्परा में माना जाता है कि …` / `tradition holds that …` / `परंपरा में मानल जाला कि …`, or `⟨text⟩ की फलश्रुति कहती है …` with the locator. No cure/heal/prevent/treat verbs, no Schedule disease names as objects of a mantra's curative verb, no "guaranteed/100 %/scientifically proven"; "उपाय/remedy" only as a translation of what a source says; health/pregnancy/grief pages carry the one-line "religious tradition, not medical advice" note in all three languages; astrology pages state once that the site describes observances and computes nothing. Phalashruti verses are reproduced as the text's own statement, labelled `फलश्रुति — ग्रन्थ का अपना कथन` (R4 §5.2, s.14(b) INFERENCE). Lint regex seeds: R4 §5.4.

2. **bhoRetroflexN** — ण → न in everyday/tadbhava Bhojpuri words (कारन, परिनाम, चरन, उदाहरन, परनाम); ण kept in mantra words, deity names and shastric terms (गणेश, नारायण, पुराण, ब्राह्मण, पूर्णाहुति, तर्पण).
3. **bhoNukta** — Bhojpuri nukta only in ड़/ढ़ (जरूरत, तारीख, आवाज; never ज़ फ़ ख़).
4. **hiNukta** — Hindi ज़/फ़ where the standard pronunciation has z/f (ज़रूरत, फ़ोन); ख़ only in तारीख़ to agree with Shubhvela; no क़/ग़. Hindi anusvara per IS 16500 §2.6.1.2 (पंडित, संकल्प, संस्कार, मंत्र — conjunct forms only inside the Sanskrit `devanagari` field) (R5 H2).
5. **digits** — Latin digits in hi/bho/en UI and prose; Devanagari digits only inside the `devanagari` field when the source has them.
6. **honorific** — deities and planets in the respectful plural (hi … हैं; bho … बाड़न); "जी" after a deity name in family prose, not inside mantra glosses.
Plus: Bhojpuri imperatives always -ीं (करीं, देखीं, सुनीं, बनीं); no Hindi ergative ने; `forbidden-hi-in-bho.json` (73 hard / 55 soft / 18 allow / 12 Latin-allow / 7 patterns) runs on every `bho` prose field with the tokenisation contract in its `_meta` (R5 §8.2); every Bhojpuri record keeps `review: "draft"`.
