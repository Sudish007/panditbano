## King Tungadhvaja and the cowherds' prasad

Suta said — sages, hear one more [[katha]]. There was a king named Tungadhvaja, devoted to the care of his people. The text says at the outset why sorrow came to him — he spurned the [[prasad]] of Satyadeva.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-1}}

Once the king was returning from the hunt in the forest. Under a banyan tree he saw cowherds with their kinsfolk performing the worship of Satyadeva, contented and full of devotion.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-3}}

The king saw it, but out of pride he neither went over nor bowed to the Lord. The cowherds still brought the prasad and set it down before the king, then went back and ate their fill of it themselves. The king spurned that prasad — and, the text says, from this his sorrow came.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-5}}

His hundred sons perished and all his wealth and grain was lost. The king understood — it is Satyadeva who has done all this; I will go to the very place where he was being worshipped.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-6}}

The king went to the cowherds and, with devotion and faith, performed the [[puja]] of Satyadeva together with them according to the [[vidhi]]. The text says that by the grace of Satyadeva he was once more blessed with wealth and sons, enjoyed happiness in this world and at the end went to Satyapura.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-8}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-9}}

## The phalashruti — the text's own statement

From here to verse 17 the katha states its own fruit. This is the [[phalashruti]] — the text's own statement, which the pandit reads at the close of the katha. The site shows these verses as exactly that and does not state them as fact; tradition holds that these fruits come from keeping the vrat and hearing the katha.

The text says — whoever performs this rare and precious vow of Satya and listens with devotion to this holy katha gains wealth and grain by the grace of Satyadeva; the poor man finds riches, the one in bonds is freed from his bonds, the fearful is released from fear, and having enjoyed the fruit he desired he goes at the end to Satyapura.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-10}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-11}}

Suta then addresses the rishis again — Brahmins, thus I have told you the [[vrat]] of Satyanarayan, by performing which a man is freed from all sorrows.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-13}}

## The names of Satyadeva in the Kali age

One part of the phalashruti explains why this god has so many names. The text says — in the Kali age the worship of Satyadeva bears fruit especially; some will call that same Lord "Kala", some "Satya", some "Isha", some "Satyanarayan" and others "Satyadeva". Taking many forms and granting everyone's wishes, that same eternal Lord will be present in the Kali age in the form of the vow of Satya — a form assumed by [[vishnu]] himself.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-14}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-15}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-16}}

The last verse of the phalashruti gives the fruit of reading and hearing — whoever reads or hears this every day, by the grace of Satyadeva his sins are destroyed. This too is the text's own statement.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-17}}

## The later births of the story's characters

Finally Suta tells who those who kept this vrat in former times became in their next lives. The list takes the characters in the order in which they appeared in the katha, and ties them to the larger world of the [[puran]] tradition and the epics. The site shows these verses too as phalashruti — they are the katha's own way of stating the fruit of the vrat across births.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-18}}

The poor [[brahman]] of Kashi — the katha names him here for the first time, Shatananda — was born as Sudama, Shri Krishna's boyhood friend, whose story is told in the [Bhagavata Purana](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=41577), book 10, chapters 80–81; in that life he meditated on Shri Krishna and attained [[moksha]].

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-19}}

The woodcutter became Guha, king of the Bhils — the Nishada chief of Shringaverapura who received Shri Rama on his way to the forest ([Valmiki Ramayana](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=1366), Ayodhya-kanda 50–52); serving Shri Rama, he went to liberation.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-20}}

King Ulkamukha became King Dasharatha, who worshipped Shri Ranganatha and attained Vaikuntha. Shri Ranganatha is Vishnu reclining on Shesha; tradition holds that the Ranganatha image was the family deity of the Ikshvaku line, given by Shri Rama to Vibhishana — which is why the katha makes Dasharatha a worshipper of Ranganatha.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-21}}

The righteous merchant Sadhu, true to his word, became King Moradhvaja, who had half his body sawn off and gave it away, and so attained liberation — the story is told in the [Jaiminiya Ashvamedha-parva](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=89684), where Shri Krishna and Arjuna, disguised as Brahmins, ask the king for half of his body.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-22}}

King Tungadhvaja became Svayambhuva Manu — the son of Brahma, the first of the Manus — and, having made everyone a devotee of the Lord, went to Vaikuntha.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-23}}

And the cowherds who worshipped under the banyan tree became the cowherds of Vraja — Shri Krishna's Vraja — who slew the demons and reached Goloka. Here the fifth adhyaya ends, and with it the five-adhyaya Satyanarayan vrat katha is complete.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-5-24}}

## What to take from this adhyaya

- The katha closes by showing two failures over the prasad — Kalavati's haste in the fourth adhyaya, King Tungadhvaja's pride here. From this comes the custom at the end of the katha: everyone takes prasad, nobody refuses it, and prasad is sent to the homes of those who could not come. The prasad-distribution step of the [Satyanarayan puja procedure](/occasions/satyanarayan-puja/) rests on this.
- The cowherds' episode shows who may perform the puja — herdsmen under a banyan tree, with no pandit and no temple. In the katha the right to the vrat is not limited to any class.
- The phalashruti (verses 10–17) is the text's own statement. In the puja procedure the pandit reads it after the katha and before the aarti; the site shows it with the label "the text's own statement" and attaches no claim of results to it.
- "Kala, Satya, Isha, Satyanarayan, Satyadeva" — these names in verses 14–15 explain why the same god is called both Satyadeva and Satyanarayan in the katha, and verse 16 calls him a form of Vishnu. That is why this puja has the structure of a Vishnu puja.
- The list of later births ties the katha to the tradition of the [Ramayana](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=14186), the [Bhagavata](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=7950) and the [Mahabharata](https://sa.wikisource.org/?curid=1962) — the katha's own claim that this vrat stood behind those great figures too. The identifications are the katha's; the name Shatananda occurs only here.
- The five-adhyaya katha is the form of the printed pothis; a seven-adhyaya recension also circulates (see the note on the katha page). In the puja, "Satyanarayan Bhagavan ki jai" after each adhyaya, and after the fifth the phalashruti, the aarti and the prasad — that order is given in the puja procedure.
