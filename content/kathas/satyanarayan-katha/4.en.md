## A question on the water — "what is in your boat?"

Released from King Chandraketu's prison, the merchant Sadhu opened his journey with auspicious rites, gave wealth to the Brahmins and set off for his own city with his son-in-law. They had gone only a little way when Lord Satyanarayan came to the boat in the guise of a dandi — a renunciant carrying a staff — and asked: Sadhu, what is there in your boat?

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-2}}

Drunk with their wealth, the two merchants laughed in mockery — why do you ask, O dandi? Do you want to carry off our money? There is nothing in the boat but creepers and leaves.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-3}}

Hearing this harsh, false answer, the dandi said only — "let your words be true" — and, going some distance, sat down by the shore of the sea.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-4}}

## Creepers and leaves; taking refuge

When the dandi had gone, Sadhu finished his daily rites. Then he saw the boat riding high on the water, as if it had been emptied. Inside, where the wealth had been, there really were creepers and leaves. Sadhu fainted and fell; when he came to, he sank into worry.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-7}}

His son-in-law steadied him — why grieve? This is the dandi's curse; he can do anything, there is no doubt of it. Come, let us take refuge with him; only then will things come right.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-9}}

Sadhu went to the dandi, bowed to him with devotion and, bowing again and again, said — whatever I said in your presence, forgive me that offence. As he spoke he was overcome with grief.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-11}}

The dandi said — do not weep; listen to me. You had turned your face away from my [[puja]]; by my command you have met with sorrow again and again. Hearing this, Sadhu understood that the Lord himself stood before him, and began to sing his praise.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-13}}

## Sadhu's hymn

Sadhu said — Lord, all the gods, Brahma and the rest, are bewildered by your maya; they do not know your qualities or your form — such is your wonder. I am a fool, deluded by your maya; how could I have known you? Be gracious; I shall worship you as fully as my means allow. Make my wealth as it was; I have come to you for refuge — save me.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-15}}

Hearing these words full of devotion, Janardana was pleased, granted the boon he wished for and vanished on the spot. Sadhu climbed into the boat and found it filled with wealth. "By the grace of Satyadeva my wish is fulfilled," he said, performed the puja with his kinsfolk according to the rule, readied the boat with a glad heart and set out for his own country.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-17}}

## Near Ratnapuri — the vrat under way at home

Close to his city Sadhu said to his son-in-law — look, there is my Ratnapuri! (This is Sadhu's own city; the Ratnasarapura of the third adhyaya was King Chandraketu's.) He sent the keeper of his wealth ahead as messenger. In the city the messenger found Sadhu's wife and, with folded hands, gave her the good news — the merchant has arrived near the city with his son-in-law, his kinsfolk and much wealth.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-20}}

At that moment Lilavati and Kalavati were keeping the [[vrat]] of Satyadeva at home. Overjoyed at the news, Lilavati completed the puja of Satyadeva and said to her daughter — I am going to see your father; come quickly too.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-24}}

## The prasad left behind

The [[kanya]] Kalavati did finish the puja of the vrat and bring it to a close, but she ran off to see her husband without taking the [[prasad]]. The text says that at this Satyadeva was angered and sank her husband in the water together with the boat and its wealth.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-25}}

Reaching the shore, Kalavati did not see her husband and fell to the ground weeping with grief. Seeing the boat in that state and the girl's distress, Sadhu and all the boatmen were struck with fear and worry. Lilavati lamented — how has he just now vanished together with the boat? By slighting which god has this happened? And then she said herself — who can fathom the greatness of Satyadeva!

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-30}}

Having lost her husband, Kalavati took up his sandals and resolved to follow him into the next world. This is the story's narrative — the katha neither praises it nor recommends it, and the site tells it only as an episode of the story.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-33}}

## A voice from the sky — "eat the prasad and come back"

Seeing his daughter's resolve, the merchant, who knew dharma, burned with grief together with his wife and began to think, and understood — it is Satyadeva who has taken him; I am deluded by his maya. He made a [[sankalp]] to worship Satyadeva as fully as his means allowed, called everyone together, told them his intent, and bowed again and again, prostrate on the ground.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-36}}

Satyadeva, protector of the lowly and loving to his devotees, was pleased, and his words were heard from the sky — your daughter left the prasad behind and came to see her husband; that is why her husband has disappeared. If she goes home, takes the prasad and comes back, she will have her husband again — there is no doubt of it.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-38}}

Hearing these words from the vault of the sky, Kalavati went home at once and took the prasad. When she came back, her husband was there before her. She said to her father — now come home; why do you delay?

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-40}}

## Home — and then every purnima

Sadhu was content. He performed the worship of Satyadeva with full rite and rule and went home with his wealth and his kinsfolk. The text says that from then on he worshipped Satyadeva on every [[purnima]] and every [[sankranti]], enjoyed happiness in this world and at the end went to Satyapura, the realm of Satyadeva.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-43}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-4-44}}

## What to take from this adhyaya

- "Never leave the prasad" — the pandit says this line at the close of every Satyanarayan katha, and this adhyaya is its source. On the [Satyanarayan puja procedure](/occasions/satyanarayan-puja/) page the custom of giving everyone prasad after the katha, so that nobody leaves the house without it, rests on this episode.
- Kalavati's mistake is haste, not disrespect — she completes the puja but runs off without the prasad. The katha's point is that the puja ends with taking the prasad; getting up before that leaves it unfinished.
- The dandi episode is a story of testing — "what is in your boat?" was a plain question, and the answer was a lie. Falling from the truth in the pride of wealth, then finding forgiveness by taking refuge — that is the adhyaya's second message, and "satyam bhavatu te vachah" (let your words be true) is its most memorable line.
- Sadhu's hymn (verses 14–16) is the only extended prayer in the katha — a devotee's words of surrender; it is the emotional centre of this adhyaya.
- Worship on every purnima and sankranti (verse 43) is the katha's own link to the custom of the monthly Satyanarayan katha; the katha prescribes no particular tithi — that is the tradition's choice.
- The sunken boat and the returning husband, Kalavati's resolve to follow with his sandals — all of this is the story's narrative. The site neither recommends any practice from it nor attaches any claim of results to it; tradition holds that honouring the prasad is part of the vrat, and that is all.
