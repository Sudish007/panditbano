## The question in the Naimisha forest

The [[katha]] opens where most of our [[puran]] texts open — in the Naimisha forest. Shaunaka and the other rishis sit with Suta, the knower of the Puranas, and put a plain question to him: which [[vrat]] or austerity brings a person the fruit he wishes for?

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-2}}

Suta does not answer in his own words. He says that the divine sage Narada once put the very same question to Vishnu, and that he will relate what the husband of Kamala replied. So a second story opens inside the first — the dialogue of Suta and the rishis, and inside it the dialogue of Narada and Narayana.

## Narada's worry

Wandering the worlds out of a wish to do good to others, the yogi Narada once came to the mortal world, the earth of men. Here he saw people beset with every kind of suffering; through their own deeds they were born into many wombs and were tormented. One question rose in his mind — what means could end their sorrow for certain? Thinking this, he went to the world of Vishnu.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-6}}

There he saw Narayana — fair in colour, four-armed, holding the conch, the discus, the mace and the lotus, a garland of forest flowers at his neck. Narada praised him: you are beyond speech and mind, your power has no end; you have no beginning, middle or end; beyond the qualities, you are yet the self of all qualities, the first cause of everything, the remover of your devotees' distress.

Hearing the hymn, the Lord asked why he had come and what was on his mind — tell me, and I shall explain it all. Narada described the condition of human beings — many afflictions, many wombs, the scorching of their own sins — and asked that, if the Lord had grace for him, he teach some simple means by which this suffering could be quieted.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-12}}

## The Lord's answer — a simple vrat

The Lord praised the question; it had been asked out of a wish to help the people. Then he said that there is a vrat of great merit, rare both in heaven and in the mortal world, and that out of affection he would now make it known.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-14}}

That vrat is the vrat of [[satyanarayan]]. The text states its fruit in its own words — whoever performs it in the proper way enjoys happiness here at once and attains liberation hereafter. The statement is the text's own; the site shows it as [[phalashruti]], the scripture's own claim.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-15}}

Narada asked further: what is the fruit of this vrat, what is its procedure, who performed it before, and when should it be done? The Lord's reply is the rest of the adhyaya. On the fruit, the text says that the vrat quiets sorrow and grief, increases wealth and grain, bestows good fortune and progeny and grants victory everywhere — all of these are the text's own words, which the tradition repeats with faith.

## The rule of the vrat — in the text's own words

Here the katha lays down what the whole [Satyanarayan puja procedure](/occasions/satyanarayan-puja/) rests on. The day may be any day — whichever day a person is filled with devotion and faith. The time is dusk. And it is not a solitary act: the god Satyanarayan is to be worshipped in the company of Brahmins and kinsfolk, intent on dharma.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-19}}

Then comes the rule of the [[naivedya]], which has become the signature of every Satyanarayan puja in the Hindi belt — the sapada measure, "sava" in Hindi, one and a quarter. Banana, [[ghee]], [[doodh]] and wheat flour; rice flour if there is no wheat; with [[shakkar]] or [[gud]]. All of these, in the sapada measure, are mixed together and offered. This mixture is what is known today as [[panjiri]].

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-20}}

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-21}}

The text also gives the order of what follows: the katha is heard together with the people, [[dakshina]] is given to the Brahmin, and then the Brahmins are fed along with one's kinsfolk.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-22}}

At the end the [[prasad]] is eaten with devotion, there is song and dance, and everyone goes home remembering Satyanarayan.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-23}}

The last verse of the adhyaya returns to the fruit and adds one particular remark — in the Kali age this is an easy means available on earth. That sentence is the key to the vrat's popularity: no long ritual, no hard rules; an evening hour, the people of the house, a sapada measure of prasad and a story.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-1-24}}

## What to take from this adhyaya

- The frame of the puja comes from inside the story — the evening hour, the presence of Brahmins and family, the sapada naivedya, hearing the katha, dakshina, the meal, the prasad. Every step of the [Satyanarayan puja procedure](/occasions/satyanarayan-puja/) is tied to these verses (18–23).
- "Sava" is said only of the naivedya; keeping the other materials in sava measures too is local custom, not the text's rule.
- All the statements of fruit (verses 15, 17–18, 24) are the text's own. Tradition holds that this vrat, performed with faith, fulfils one's wishes; the site states this as the tradition's belief and nothing more.
- The closing line of the adhyaya calls it the first adhyaya of the Satyanarayan vrat katha in the Reva Khanda of the Skanda Purana; what scholars make of that claim is set out in the introduction to the [Sanskrit text](/texts/satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit/).
