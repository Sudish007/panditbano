## The first keeper of the vrat — the poor Brahmin of Kashi

At the end of the first adhyaya the rishis had wanted to know who performed this vrat before anyone else. Suta now tells them. In the lovely city of Kashi there lived a [[brahman]] who was utterly poor. Tormented by hunger and thirst, he wandered the earth day after day.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-1}}

The Lord, who holds Brahmins dear, saw that suffering Brahmin. He came to him in the form of an old Brahmin and asked respectfully — Brahmin, why do you wander the earth every day in such misery? I wish to hear it all.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-3}}

The Brahmin told his story — I am a Brahmin in utter poverty, and I roam begging for alms; if you know a way, please be kind and tell me. The old Brahmin replied: Satyanarayan is [[vishnu]] himself, the giver of the fruits one wishes for. Worship him and keep this excellent vrat. Here the text states the vrat's fruit in its own words — by doing it a person is freed from all sorrows; that is the scripture's own claim.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-5}}

The Lord in the old man's form carefully explained the whole procedure of the vrat and vanished on the spot. The Brahmin got no sleep that night — one thought kept turning in his mind: the vrat that Brahmin described, I shall perform.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-8}}

Rising in the morning he made his [[sankalp]] — "I will perform the Satyanarayan vrat" — and went out to beg. The text says that on that very day he received abundant wealth, and with it he performed the vrat of Satyadeva together with his kinsfolk.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-10}}

By the power of the vrat that Brahmin was freed from all sorrows and became prosperous — so the text says. From then on he kept the vrat month after month, and, the text says, in the end he attained the rare liberation. Here Suta repeats to the rishis a sentence that Narayana had spoken to Narada — whenever a person on earth performs this vrat, at that very moment his sorrow is destroyed. This too is [[phalashruti]], the scripture's own statement.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-12}}

## The rishis' next question

Suta said — that I have told you; what more shall I say? The rishis' faith had grown. They asked: who else on earth heard of it from that Brahmin and practised it? We wish to hear all of it.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-15}}

## The second keeper — the woodcutter

Suta related: once that same excellent Brahmin was performing the vrat with his kinsfolk and family, on the scale his means allowed. Just then a woodcutter arrived. He set his wood down outside and, parched with thirst, came into the Brahmin's house. Seeing the Brahmin engaged in the vrat, he bowed and asked — what is this that you are doing? What does one gain by doing it? Tell me in detail.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-19}}

The Brahmin answered — this is the vrat of Satyanarayan, which grants everything one desires; by his grace alone I have gained all this wealth and grain.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-20}}

Learning of the vrat, the woodcutter was overjoyed. He drank the [[charanamrit]], ate the [[prasad]] and set off for the town. On the way he kept his mind on the god Satyanarayan and bound himself to a resolve — whatever money I get today from selling wood in the village, with that very money I shall perform the excellent vrat of Satyadeva.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-23}}

With the wood on his head he went to the fine town where the wealthy lived. The text says that on that day he received twice the usual price for his wood. Glad at heart, he took ripe bananas, [[shakkar]], [[ghee]], [[doodh]] and wheat flour — the same five things named for the [[naivedya]] in the first adhyaya — made them up together in the sapada measure and went home. Then he called his kinsfolk and performed the vrat according to the rule.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-26}}

The last verse of the adhyaya gives the woodcutter's fruit — by the power of the vrat he came to have wealth and sons, enjoyed happiness in this world and at the end went to Satyapura, the Lord's abode. That is the text's own statement.

{{verse:satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit-2-27}}

## What to take from this adhyaya

- The first two keepers of the vrat are the plainest people in society — a Brahmin living on alms and a woodcutter. The vrat needs neither wealth nor rank; the woodcutter performs it with a single day's earnings. That is the message of the vrat, and the reason it became a vrat of every household.
- The woodcutter's episode shows two practices that continue today — arriving at a house where the vrat is under way and taking charanamrit and prasad, and binding oneself, after taking prasad, to perform the vrat in turn.
- The naivedya ingredients are listed here a second time (verses 25–26): banana, sugar, ghee, milk, wheat flour — in the sapada measure. The [[panjiri]] of the [Satyanarayan puja procedure](/occasions/satyanarayan-puja/) takes its ingredients from these verses.
- The statements of fruit (verses 6, 11, 13–14, 27) are the text's own. Tradition holds that this vrat removes sorrow; the site states this as the tradition's belief and nothing more.
- "The vrat every month" (verse 12) — this is where the custom of the monthly Satyanarayan katha on the purnima attaches; the tithi itself is not named in the text, it is the tradition's choice.
