Pandit Bano is a free reference site that puts the [[vidhi]], the [[mantra]] and the [[katha]] of every North Indian family occasion in one place. Each occasion is given as a complete procedure: what to prepare, who does what, what is said and done in which order, and what every [[mantra]] means. All explanations were written by the site's authors; every page lists its sources.

The site currently holds {{count:occasions}} occasions, {{count:mantras}} mantras, {{count:verses}} verses, {{count:kathas}} kathas, {{count:lessons}} lessons and {{count:terms}} glossary terms. These numbers are not typed by hand; the site counts them from its own data every time it is built.

# What the site gives you

- **Occasions** — one page for every household [[sanskar]], [[puja]], [[vrat]], festival and shanti rite: an overview, the timing (the [[muhurat]] rules, with a link to [Shubhvela](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/) for the dates), the roles, a printable and tickable [[samagri]] checklist, every step in order — its action, its mantras and the line the [[pandit]] speaks — then the regional variants, questions and answers, and sources.
- **Pothi mode** — the same occasion in large type with no decoration, mantras and actions only, for printing and for saving offline on a phone.
- **Mantras and texts** — every mantra and verse in Devanagari (with [[svara]] wherever the source has them), in two Roman transliterations, with a word-by-word split and glosses, then the prose meaning, an explanation, the [[vinyog]] — which step of which rite it is spoken in — and a source line. A button plays the text in the browser's own voice — an approximate machine voice, not Vedic recitation.
- **Kathas** — from the Satyanarayan katha to every vrat katha, retold in the site's own words, with the Sanskrit verses where an original exists and the Purana chapter cited.
- **Learn** — a course towards becoming a purohit: from Level 0 (orientation) to Level 4 (the long rites such as shraddha, antyeshti, the wedding and Rudrabhishek), each lesson with a self-check quiz and pronunciation practice.
- **Glossary and reference** — the vocabulary of ritual in three languages; [the sankalpa template, the muhurat rules, the bibliography and the verification report](/reference/).

# Who it is for

Three kinds of reader can work from the same page:

1. **The learner** who wants to become a pandit and understand the sequence from the beginning.
2. **The [[yajman]]**, the family hosting a puja at home, who wants to know what the pandit is saying and what to keep ready.
3. **The practising pandit** who wants [[pothi]] on the phone — clean, in large type, opening without an internet connection.

# Which tradition

The site takes the Vajasaneyi Madhyandina branch of the Shukla Yajurveda and its household manual, the [Paraskara Grihya Sutra](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%97%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D), as its baseline, because most family purohits of the Hindi belt belong to this tradition. Every procedure shows three layers separately: the Vedic core (the sequence of the grihya-sutra and the mantras of the Samhita), the [[paddhati]] layer (the Puranic frame of Ganesha-Ambika worship, [[kalash-sthapana]], the matrikas, the nine planets, the [[nandi-shraddha]]) and local custom (tilak, the garland exchange, sindoor, the kohbar and other family and regional practices). Regional differences are called variants, never errors — the grihya-sutra itself says that at weddings and funerals the word of the village is the authority.

# Three languages

The site is in Hindi, English and Bhojpuri; its Bhojpuri name is **पंडित बनीं**. Sanskrit mantras appear in the same Devanagari text in all three; their meaning and explanation change with the chosen language. Hindi and Bhojpuri spellings are matched to the term bank of [Shubhvela](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/) so that the two sites speak one orthography. The Bhojpuri text was written by the site's authors and is awaiting review by native readers; every page says so in its footer.

# Rules of honesty

- The site makes no claim of its own about what a puja or a mantra brings; where the tradition says something, the page says "tradition holds". No medical, financial or legal outcome is promised.
- Where a page touches health, grief, children or astrology, one line reminds the reader that this is a description of religious tradition.
- The text of every mantra was taken from a named digital source, linked on the same line; no mantra was typed from memory. The full policy is on the [Sources](/sources/) page.
- Every number shown on the site is counted from the data. The browser voice is labelled approximate.
- How the site was checked, and what has not been checked yet, is written on the [Verification](/reference/verification/) page.

# What the site is not

The site is no [[panchang]]: tithi, [[nakshatra]] and muhurat dates are not computed here; for those, every page links to [Shubhvela](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/). It is not a pandit-booking service, not a shop for astrological remedies, and not an app-store app. There are no advertisements, no accounts, no cookies and no third-party requests; even the fonts are served from the site itself. The [Privacy](/privacy/) page says the whole of it in one screen.

# How it was built

The site is a set of static pages and runs free of charge on [GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com/). The code is open under the [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) licence; the explanations written for the site are under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); the mantra text is in the public domain, and where it was taken from Wikisource the Wikisource CC BY-SA 4.0 notice travels with it. The type is set in [Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Tiro+Devanagari+Sanskrit) for the mantras and in [Noto Sans Devanagari](https://fonts.google.com/noto/specimen/Noto+Sans+Devanagari) and [Noto Sans](https://fonts.google.com/noto/specimen/Noto+Sans) for the prose — all three are open fonts under the [OFL](https://openfontlicense.org/). The full [bibliography](/reference/bibliography/) is a page of its own.
