This site reproduces all traditional texts in full; the source of each is stated on the record itself. The original text of every mantra, verse and line was taken from a named digital source — no mantra was typed from memory — and wherever a second digital source existed, the reading was checked against it. The meanings, explanations, katha retellings and the Hindi, English and Bhojpuri procedure text were written by the site's authors; no publisher's commentary or translation was copied.

The source registry at the foot of this page is generated from the data every time the site is built, grouped by project; each project's page gives the name of every source, its address, the date it was retrieved and its licence line. The complete list of books, with links to publishers and courses, is on the [Bibliography](/reference/bibliography/) page.

# How every record states its source

Under every mantra or verse there is a source line in small type. It takes one of these forms:

- **Source text** — the name and link of the page the Devanagari was taken from, the date of retrieval, and whether the text is unchanged or what was normalised (punctuation, the avagraha, a visarga typed as a colon). Variant readings, where they exist, are shown separately below the verse.
- **Checked against** — the second digital source the reading was compared with.
- **Sequence facts** — modern editions (such as the Gita Press procedure manuals) from which only the order of a rite was cross-checked; no text from them appears here.
- **Transcribed from** — where the text was transcribed from the scan of a public-domain printed edition: the edition, publisher, year and scan address, and the second source it was verified against.
- **Recitation reference** — where a portal is linked only for listening or comparison (for example the [Vedic Heritage Portal](https://vedicheritage.gov.in/samhitas/yajurveda/vajasneyi-madhyandina-samhita/)), a link alone.

Each record also states whether the underlying work is in the public domain, in copyright or of uncertain status, and on what basis. Where the source carries [[svara]], they are kept; where it does not, the page says that the accent marks are not shown.

# What the site wrote itself

The meanings, explanations, procedures, katha retellings, lessons and glossary — in all three languages — were written by the site's authors and are released under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), so that they combine cleanly with text that came from Wikisource. The code of the site is open under the [MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) licence. The Devanagari text of the mantras is in the public domain; where it came from Wikisource, the CC BY-SA 4.0 notice for the Wikisource transcription travels with it.

# Corrections

If you find a mistake in a text, say so on [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/Sudish007/panditbano/issues); the full checking process is described on the [Verification](/reference/verification/) page.
