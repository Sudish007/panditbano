# Three kinds of book

A Hindi-belt purohit works from three kinds of text, and the pages of this site cite all three.

**1. The Samhita — the mantras.** The [Vajasaneyi Madhyandina Samhita](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%9C%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%83) of the Shukla [[yajurved]] is where the Vedic [[mantra]] of every rite comes from: the Gayatri, the purnahuti, the mantras of the kalash, the [[havan]] and the [[vivah]]. The site cites it as "VS adhyaya.mantra" (VS 36.3 is the Gayatri), copies the text from [Sanskrit Wikisource](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%9C%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%83) with its svara marks, and checks it against the [TITUS](https://titus.uni-frankfurt.de/texte/etcs/ind/aind/ved/yvw/vs/vs.htm) edition.

**2. The sutra — the sequence.** The [Paraskara Grihya Sutra](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%97%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D) is the [[grihya-sutra]] of this Veda: a short Sanskrit text in three kandas and fifty-two kandikas that fixes the order of the household rites — the wedding (kanda 1), the childhood [[sanskar]] and the thread ceremony (kanda 2), house-building, the rite for the dead and the shraddha (kanda 3). It gives the bare instruction and the first words of the mantra; the handbooks build the full procedure around it. Hermann Oldenberg's English translation of 1886 is in the public domain ([Sacred Books of the East, volume 29](https://archive.org/details/india.history.resource.89728)); the site checks every sutra it quotes against it. **3. The paddhati — the full procedure.** A [[paddhati]] is a handbook that compiles the whole rite in order: the sutra's steps, the Samhita's mantras in full, the Puranic frame ([[ganesh-ambika-pujan]], [[kalash-sthapana]], the [[matrika]], the [[navagraha]], the [[nandi-shraddha]]), the worded [[sankalp]] with blanks for the name and the gotra, the [[samagri]] and the instructions in Hindi. The pandit's [[pothi]] is a paddhati — printed or handwritten.

# The Gita Press shelf

The handbooks most Hindi-belt pandits own are the Gita Press, Gorakhpur editions. The site cites them by title and catalogue code (the number printed on the Gita Press page) and uses them for sequence facts only — their Hindi prose is in copyright and is never copied. The shelf, as the [bibliography](/reference/bibliography/) lists it:

- [Nitya Karma Puja Prakash](https://gitapress.org/product/nitya-karma-pooja-prakash-hindi-gita-press-gorakhpur-publication) (592) — [[sandhya]], [[panchopachar]] and [[shodashopachar]] puja, the [[nitya-karma]].
- [Nitya Karma Prayoga](https://gitapress.org/product/nitya-karma-prayoga-a-complete-guide-to-daily-hindu-rituals-with-mantras-and-procedures-by-or-language-hindi-or-code-139) (139) — the shorter daily manual.
- [Sanskar Prakash](https://gitapress.org/product/sanskar-prakash-hindi) (2033) — the samskaras from jatakarma to samavartana.
- [Vivah Sanskar Paddhati](https://gitapress.org/product/vivah-sanskar-paddhati-hindi) (2191) and [Upanayan Sanskar Paddhati](https://gitapress.org/product/upnayan-sanskar-padhati-hindi) (2183) — the two single-rite handbooks.
- [Antyakarma-Shraddha Prakash](https://gitapress.org/product/antyakarm-saradh-prakash-or-language-hindi-or-code-1593) (1593) and [Tripindi Shraddha Paddhati](https://gitapress.org/product/tripindi-shradhpadhati-hindi) (1928) — the rites of death and of the ancestors.
- [Satyanarayan Vrat Katha](https://gitapress.org/product/sri-satya-narayana-vrata-katha-hindi) (1367), [Rudrashtadhyayi](https://gitapress.org/product/rudrastadhyayee-hindi) (1627), [Durga Saptashati](https://gitapress.org/product/durga-saptashati-or-language-sanskrit-or-code-876) (876) and [Garud Puran Saroddhar](https://gitapress.org/product/garud-puran-saroddhar-with-simplified-commentary-or-sacred-hindu-scripture-on-dharma-afterlife-and-liberation-or-language-hindi-or-size-medium-or-code-1416) (1416) — the texts recited at the vrat, the [[rudrabhishek]], the Chandi path and the days after a death.
- [Panchang Pujan Paddhati](https://gitapress.org/product/panchang-poojan-paddhati-hindi) (2228) — the worship of the five limbs of the day.

The presses of Varanasi, Mumbai and Gorakhpur print many more pothis. The wedding sequence on this site follows one of them, the [Vivah Sanskar Paddhati of Acharya Akhilesh Dwivedi](https://archive.org/details/mvf_20240803_20240803_1558) (Mumbai, 2020), whose index marks the lokachara items as lokachara. Two more deserve a name because learners meet them online: the [Karmakand Bhaskar](https://www.awgp.org/en/literature/book/kram_kand_bhaskar/v1) and the [Karmakand Pradeep](https://www.epustakalaya.com/book/karmakanda-pradeep) found on the web are Gayatri Pariwar editions — a reform paddhati, not the Pauranik Madhyandina pothi — while the [Karmakand Pradeep: Dashakarmadi Paddhati](https://www.astrodivines.com/books/karmakand-pradeep/) of Acharya Devnarayan Sharma (Bharatiya Vidya Prakashan, Varanasi, 2024) is a traditional compilation.

# The Puranas and smritis behind the frame

The paddhati layer draws its verses from the Puranas and the smritis, and the site cites each by chapter: the Satyanarayan katha from the Reva Khanda of the [Skanda Purana](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D) ([the Sanskrit text](/texts/satyanarayan-vrat-katha-sanskrit/), [the retelling](/kathas/satyanarayan-katha/)); the rites of death and the readings of the thirteen days from the [Garuda Purana](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%A1%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D); the Durga Saptashati from the [Markandeya Purana](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%A3%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A1%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D); the rules of sutak from the [Manusmriti](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%83) and the [Yajnavalkya Smriti](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%9C%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%9E%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%B2%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%83); the [navagraha stotra](/texts/navagraha-stotra/) and the verse of the [[raksha-sutra]] from the [Bhavishya Purana](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B7%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D). Where a verse comes from a paddhati and from no Veda or Purana, the site says so and cites the handbook.

# What the pothi holds

Ask a working pandit for his pothi and you will find, in his own order: the sequence of each rite he conducts, with the steps numbered; the sankalpa texts with blanks for the samvatsara, the month, the tithi, the gotra and the names; the mantras with their [[vinyog]], often only the first words where he knows the rest; the samagri list for each occasion; the regional and family variations his yajamans keep; the customary [[dakshina]] and the timings — how long a Satyanarayan puja, a griha-pravesh or a wedding takes. The [pandit's etiquette](/occasions/pandit-shishtachar/) page describes this notebook and the working relationship around it. On this site every occasion has a **pothi mode** — the same page in large type, mantras and actions only, for printing or for the phone (for example the [wedding pothi](/occasions/vivah/pothi/)) — and the [sankalpa template](/reference/sankalpa/) gives the blanks and a worked example.

# Reading a source line

Every mantra and verse on this site carries a source line. "VS 36.3" is the Vajasaneyi Samhita, adhyaya 36, mantra 3. "PGS 1.8.1" is Paraskara, kanda 1, kandika 8, sutra 1. "RV" is a Rigveda verse by mandala, hymn and verse. A paddhati verse that stands in no Veda or Purana names the handbook in its source line; a Puranic verse names its Purana and chapter. The [verification](/reference/verification/) page explains how each text was fetched and checked, and the [bibliography](/reference/bibliography/) lists every book and edition.
