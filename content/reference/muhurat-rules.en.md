This page explains which rules a pandit weighs when choosing the time of an auspicious rite. The rules are given as tradition — the line of the grihya-sutra, the tables of the muhurta texts and the practice of the Hindi belt are shown separately. No date is computed on this site: for the [[tithi]], [[nakshatra]] and muhurat of any year, use [Shubhvela's muhurat finder](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/muhurat/), its [festival list](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/festivals/) and [today's panchang](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/panchang/new-delhi/).

# What a muhurat is

The [[muhurat]] is the chosen time — the day and the hour at which a rite is begun. To choose it, the five limbs of the [[panchang]] are read: tithi, weekday, nakshatra, yoga and karana; above them, the state of the month (which period is running), the positions of the Sun and the Moon, and the [[lagna]] of that hour. The pandit makes the final decision from the host's horoscope, the custom of the family and his own almanac; the tables below are only the general rules on which that decision rests.

# Periods in which auspicious rites are postponed

Tradition does not begin a wedding, [[upanayan]], [[grih-pravesh]] or [[mundan]] in these periods; daily puja, kathas, vrats and shraddha are untouched by them.

| Period | When | What is postponed |
|---|---|---|
| [[kharmas]] | The month or so in which the Sun stands in Dhanu (mid-December to Makar Sankranti in mid-January) and the month or so in which it stands in Meena (mid-March to mid-April) | Weddings, engagements, tonsure, the sacred thread, house-warming, a new business; the Pausha month of this kind is also called "malmas" |
| [[chaturmas]] | From [[devshayani-ekadashi]] (Ashadha, bright eleventh) to [[dev-uthani-ekadashi]] (Kartik, bright eleventh), about four months | Weddings, upanayana, house-warming, tonsure; most vrats and kathas fall in these very months, and with the Tulsi wedding on Dev Uthani the auspicious rites open again |
| [[adhik-maas]] | In a year in which a lunar month is repeated, the repeated month (roughly every third year) | New auspicious beginnings; yearly festivals and vrats are kept in the regular month of the same name, not in the added one |
| Holashtak | The eight days from Phalguna bright eighth to Holika Dahan on the full moon | Weddings, house-warming and other auspicious beginnings — especially in the practice of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh |
| [[pitru-paksha]] | The dark fortnight of Bhadrapada (Ashvin in the full-moon-ending reckoning), from the full-moon shraddha to Sarva Pitru Amavasya | New beginnings and purchases; this fortnight belongs to [[shraddha]] and tarpana |
| Jupiter or Venus "set" | When Brihaspati or Shukra stands so close to the Sun that it cannot be seen at all (the panchang marks it "asta" and later "udaya") | Weddings, upanayana, house-warming — the muhurta texts make the visibility of both planets a condition of a wedding |

Some hours within the day are also left out: [[rahu-kaal]] (an eighth of the daytime, chosen by the weekday), [[bhadra]] (the span of the Vishti karana, when the Moon is in certain signs) and [[panchak]] (the five days or so in which the Moon moves from the second half of Dhanishtha to Revati, in which tradition does not begin five kinds of work such as roofing, making a bed, travelling south and cremation). Their timing changes every day, so read them from the panchang.

# Rules by occasion

## The wedding

- **The rule of the grihya-sutra.** The [Paraskara Grihya Sutra](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%97%E0%A5%83%E0%A4%B9%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%82%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D) (1.4.5) says: "उदगयन आपूर्यमाणपक्षे पुण्याहे कुमार्याः पाणिं गृह्णीयात्" — in the [[uttarayan]], in the waxing [[shukla-paksha]], on an auspicious day, let him take the girl's hand. This is the oldest rule; the later tradition added the details below.
- **The rule of the muhurta text.** By the wedding chapter of the [Muhurta Chintamani](https://archive.org/details/muhurt-chintamani), a [[vivah]] takes the tithis 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12 and 13 of either fortnight; eleven nakshatras — Rohini, Mrigashira, Magha, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha, Mula, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada and Revati; the weekdays Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday; by day or by night. The rikta tithis (4, 9, 14), the eighth, the new moon and the first are left out. Above this the pandit checks the lagna, the Moon's strength and the tara-bala, which are not given here.
- **The wedding season of the Hindi belt.** In practice weddings begin after Dev Uthani Ekadashi and run through Margashirsha; they pause in Kharmas; they resume in Magha and Phalguna up to Holashtak; they pause again in the Kharmas of Chaitra; they run in Vaishakha, Jyeshtha and Ashadha up to Devshayani Ekadashi; and they do not take place in Chaturmas. The periods in which Jupiter or Venus is set are subtracted from this, so a given year may have few wedding days.
- **Engagement, tilak, the lagna letter.** For the [[sagai]] the same wedding chapter (vagdana) applies: the tithis above plus the full moon, the wedding nakshatras, the same four weekdays.

## House-warming

- By the Vastu chapter of the [Muhurta Chintamani](https://archive.org/details/muhurt-chintamani), the griha pravesh is held by day only; tithis 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13 (leaving out the rikta 4, 9, 14, the eighth, the new moon and the first); nakshatras of the fixed and soft groups — Ashwini, Rohini, Mrigashira, Punarvasu, Pushya, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Chitra, Swati, Anuradha, Uttara Ashadha, Shravana, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati; weekdays Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday.
- For building a house the grihya-sutra says only "पुण्याहे" — on an auspicious day (Paraskara 3.4.2); the ranking of months is a later tradition and changes with the almanac followed, so it is not written here. In Chaturmas, Kharmas and an added month the house-warming is postponed.

## Tonsure (chudakarana)

- The grihya-sutra (Paraskara 2.1.1–2): "सांवत्सरिकस्य चूडाकरणम् । तृतीये वाऽप्रतिहते" — the tonsure of a one-year-old child, or in the third year, where the family custom does not forbid it. In practice families choose an odd year such as the first, third or fifth; that is a folk rule beyond the grihya-sutra.
- The samskara chapter of the [Muhurta Chintamani](https://archive.org/details/muhurt-chintamani): by day only; tithis 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 13; nakshatras Ashwini, Mrigashira, Punarvasu, Pushya, Hasta, Chitra, Swati, Jyeshtha, Shravana, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha, Revati; weekdays Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. Postponed in Chaturmas and Kharmas. Having the tonsure done at a tirtha (on the bank of the Ganga, at a Devi shrine) is folk custom, unmentioned in the grihya-sutra.

## Naming and the other rites of infancy

- **[[namkaran]].** The grihya-sutra (Paraskara 1.17.1): "दशम्यामुत्थाप्य ब्राह्मणान्भोजयित्वा पिता नाम करोति" — on the tenth day, after the mother has risen and the Brahmins have been fed, the father gives the name. In practice it falls on the eleventh or twelfth day (with the barahi) or later on a good day. By the muhurta text: by day; leaving out the rikta tithis 4, 9, 14 and the eighth and the new moon; in the soft and fixed nakshatras; on Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday. The table that gives the first letter of the name from the birth nakshatra is on the naming page.
- **[[nishkraman]].** The grihya-sutra (Paraskara 1.17.5): "चतुर्थे मासि निष्क्रमणिका" — in the fourth month the first outing and the sight of the Sun.
- **[[annaprashan]].** The grihya-sutra (Paraskara 1.19.1): "षष्ठे मासेऽन्नप्राशनम्" — in the sixth month the first grain.
- **Upanayana.** The grihya-sutra (Paraskara 2.2.1) places the upanayana of a Brahmin boy in the eighth year (counted from birth or from before it — both views are found); in practice it is held outside Chaturmas, mostly between Magha and Ashadha.
- **[[vidyarambh]].** By the muhurta text: by day; tithis 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12; the nakshatras of learning — Ashwini, Mrigashira, Ardra, Punarvasu, Pushya, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Chitra, Swati, Anuradha, Uttara Ashadha, Shravana, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha, Revati; weekdays Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. Beginning the letters on Vasant Panchami is folk practice.

## Vrats, shraddha and the lunar birthday

- **Starting a vrat.** By the [Dharmasindhu](https://archive.org/details/dharmasindhubykashinathupadhyaya1888nirnaysagarpress_498_a) and the verses on vrata-arambha: by day; tithis 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, 13 and the full moon; the soft and fixed nakshatras; weekdays Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. Each vrat's own tithi is on its page.
- **The yearly shraddha and Pitru Paksha.** The yearly shraddha falls on the tithi of passing, on the day on which that tithi covers the afternoon; in Pitru Paksha the same tithi number gives that ancestor's day.
- **[[janma-tithi]].** The anniversary of the lunar birth tithi is the day on which that tithi prevails at sunrise; an added month is skipped.
- **The last rites.** No muhurat is sought for them; only for a cremation that falls in Panchak does tradition add an extra procedure (the Panchak shanti), described on the page of the last rites.

# The limits of this page

- These are rules, not dates. Take each year's tithis, nakshatras, setting and rising of the planets and the bounds of Kharmas and Chaturmas from [Shubhvela](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/muhurat/), which scores days by these same panchang rules but does not add the lagna.
- Differences between almanacs (full-moon-ending or new-moon-ending months, the rules for deciding a tithi) and the custom of a family or region can give different answers; where the family's pandit and this page disagree, the pandit's word stands — the grihya-sutra itself says so.
- This page claims no result for any muhurat; "auspicious" and "to be avoided" are the tradition's words.
