The sankalpa is the first sentence of every puja: with water in hand the host names the time, the place, his gotra and name, and the purpose of the rite. The procedure — when, who, how — is on the [sankalpa occasion page](/occasions/sankalp/); this page is for the **blanks** of the frame. The site does not compute tithis or nakshatras; every limb is read from [Shubhvela's panchang](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/panchang/), whose day card for each city gives the tithi, nakshatra, yoga and karana with the time each ends.

# The frame

{{mantra:pad-vishnur-vishnur-vishnuh}}

The sentence has three parts. The first — from the remembrance of Vishnu to 'Bharatakhande' — is the same in every sankalpa, and only the name of one's land (state) and town is added to it. The second part is the limbs of the panchang, which belong to the day. The third is the host's identity and the rite. In place of 'amuka' (so-and-so) the real words are spoken in the Sanskrit locative — the table below gives the form of each limb and where to find it in the panchang.

# How to read each limb from a panchang

| Limb | Spoken form | Where it is found | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samvatsara (the year's name) | amuka-nama-samvatsare — e.g. 'Siddharthi-nama-samvatsare' | the first page of the panchang; the cycle of sixty names (Prabhava, Vibhava … Kshaya) | the northern name runs apart from the southern one; take the name of the panchang your region follows. Many handbooks also speak the Vikram year as a number (2083) |
| Ayana | uttarayane / dakshinayane | uttarayana from Makar Sankranti (mid-January) to Karka Sankranti (mid-July), then dakshinayana | fixed by the Sun's course, not directly by the month's name |
| Ritu (season) | vasanta-ritau / grishma-ritau / varsha-ritau / sharad-ritau / hemanta-ritau / shishira-ritau | the handbooks pair the lunar months: Chaitra–Vaishakh vasanta, Jyeshtha–Ashadh grishma, Shravan–Bhadrapad varsha, Ashvin–Kartik sharad, Margashirsha–Paush hemanta, Magh–Phalgun shishira | some panchang utilities derive the season from the Sun's sign and may name the next season — take your own panchang's name |
| Masa (month) | Kartika-mase, Margashirsha-mase … | 'month' on the day card — the purnimanta name in the Hindi belt | in the dark fortnight the purnimanta and amanta names differ; in an adhika month, 'adhika-Kartika-mase' |
| Paksha (fortnight) | shukla-pakshe / krishna-pakshe | written with the tithi | — |
| Tithi | pratipat-tithau, dvitiyayam tithau, tritiyayam, chaturthyam, panchamyam, shashthyam, saptamyam, ashtamyam, navamyam, dashamyam, ekadashyam, dvadashyam, trayodashyam, chaturdashyam, purnimayam, amavasyayam tithau | 'tithi … till' on the day card | the tithi running at the time of the puja; for a vrat or festival, the tithi the observance is for |
| Vara (weekday) | Ravi-vasare, Soma-vasare, Bhauma-vasare (Tuesday), Budha-vasare, Guru-vasare, Bhrigu-vasare (Friday), Shani-vasare | the calendar | one weekday from sunrise to the next sunrise |
| Nakshatra | Ashvini-nakshatre, Bharani-nakshatre … Revati-nakshatre (27) | 'nakshatra … till' on the day card | check the ending time — if the puja is later, the next nakshatra |
| Yoga | Vishkumbha-yoge … Vaidhriti-yoge (27) | 'yoga … till' on the day card | same rule |
| Karana | Bava-karane, Balava-karane, Kaulava-karane, Taitila-karane, Gara-karane, Vanija-karane, Vishti-karane (Bhadra), Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga, Kimstughna | 'karana … till' on the day card | one karana is half a tithi; it can change twice in a day |
| Signs of the Sun and Moon | Tula-rashi-sthite Surye, Mithuna-rashi-sthite Chandre | 'Sun · Moon' on the day card | spoken where the handbooks give it; dropped in the short sankalpa |
| Gotra | Kashyapa-gotrotpannah / Kashyapa-gotrah | the elders, the family purohit, an old wedding letter | if unknown, see below |
| Name | Ramaprasada-sharmaham / Ramaprasada-namaham | — | the handbooks print traditional suffixes after the name — Sharma, Varma, Gupta, Dasa; many families today simply speak their name, 'amuka-namaham' |
| The rite | …prityartham …pujanam / …vratam / …samskaram karishye | the page of that occasion | each occasion page carries its own purpose clause |

# A worked example

The day card of [Shubhvela's Varanasi panchang](https://sudish007.github.io/shubhvela/en/panchang/varanasi/) for Saturday 31 October 2026, sunrise 06:04: tithi krishna shashthi (till 16:57), nakshatra Ardra (till 07:12), yoga Siddha (till 17:32), karana Gara (till 06:08), the Sun in Tula, the Moon in Mithuna, Vikram 2083 · Shaka 1948, month Kartik (purnimanta). The samvatsara's name this year is Siddharthi; Kartik gives the season sharad, and the Sun is on its southward course — dakshinayana. Suppose Ramaprasad of the Kashyapa gotra makes the sankalpa of a Satyanarayan katha at sunrise:

> ॐ विष्णुर्विष्णुर्विष्णुः श्रीमद्भगवतो महापुरुषस्य विष्णोराज्ञया प्रवर्तमानस्य ब्रह्मणो द्वितीयपरार्धे श्रीश्वेतवाराहकल्पे वैवस्वतमन्वन्तरे अष्टाविंशतितमे कलियुगे कलिप्रथमचरणे जम्बूद्वीपे भरतखण्डे आर्यावर्तैकदेशे उत्तरप्रदेशदेशे वाराणसीनगरे श्रीमन्नृपतिवीरविक्रमादित्यसमयतः २०८३ संख्यापरिमिते प्रवर्तमानसंवत्सरे सिद्धार्थिनामसंवत्सरे दक्षिणायने शरदृतौ कार्तिकमासे कृष्णपक्षे षष्ठ्यां तिथौ शनिवासरे आर्द्रानक्षत्रे सिद्धयोगे गरकरणे तुलाराशिस्थिते सूर्ये मिथुनराशिस्थिते चंद्रे शेषेषु ग्रहेषु यथायथाराशिस्थानस्थितेषु सत्सु एवं ग्रहगुणगणविशेषणविशिष्टायां शुभपुण्यतिथौ काश्यपगोत्रोत्पन्नः रामप्रसादशर्माहं मम सकुटुम्बस्य सपरिवारस्य श्रुतिस्मृतिपुराणोक्तफलप्राप्त्यर्थं श्रीसत्यनारायणप्रीत्यर्थं सत्यनारायणव्रतकथाश्रवणपूजनं करिष्ये ॥

How to read it: if the same puja were at ten in the morning the tithi (shashthi) and the yoga (Siddha) would stand, but the nakshatra changes at 07:12 to Punarvasu and the karana after 06:08 to the next one — which is why the pandit looks at the panchang again at the moment of the sankalpa. The handbooks' usage: samvatsara, month, ayana and season as at sunrise; tithi, nakshatra, yoga and karana as at that hour.

# If the gotra is not known

{{mantra:pad-gotrasya-tvaparijnane}}

This rule, quoted in the dharmashastra digest [Samskararatnamala](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%BE_%28%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%83_%E0%A5%A7%29/%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%82_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D), is the one Hindi-belt pandits follow: when the gotra is unknown, 'Kashyapa gotra' is spoken. Tradition gives as the reason that the sage Kashyapa is counted as the first ancestor of all creatures. It is a convention for completing the rite, not a statement about anyone's descent; it is worth asking the elders first. The same digest's shraddha section says that when an ancestor's name is unknown the names Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva are spoken (except in pinda-dana).

# The short sankalpa of the daily rites

{{mantra:pad-atradya-mahamangalya}}

In the sandhya, the bath, japa and the daily puja the whole statement of place and time is not spoken — only the month, fortnight, tithi, weekday and purpose.

# A woman's sankalpa; the sankalpa with one's wife

The sentence stays the same; the feminine forms are used for oneself — 'Kashyapa-gotrotpanna Sitadevi aham … karishye'. Making the sankalpa together with his wife the husband says 'sapatniko'ham', and for the family 'mama sakutumbasya saparivarasya'. A married woman gives her husband's gotra and an unmarried one her father's — that is the handbooks' usage; the family's custom has the last word.
